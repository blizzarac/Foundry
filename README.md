# Ironhex

A **turn-based hex roguelike with souls-like combat**, set in a dead machine world. You are a salvaged combat frame, five sectors deep in a foundry that never shut down; everything still running down here wants you for parts. Plain HTML5 canvas, zero dependencies, plays on desktop and phones.

**Play it:** open `index.html`, or via GitHub Pages once enabled for this repo.

## The one rule

Combat is **deterministic and fully telegraphed**. Every machine shows exactly which hexes it will strike next cycle — red means *don't be standing there*. There is no damage RNG anywhere. Every death is a misread, never a dice roll. That's where the skill lives.

## The toolkit

- **Power** drives strikes (1), dashes (2) and deflects (2). Moving restores 1, venting heat restores 2. Overdraw your capacitor and all you can do is walk.
- **Dash** to any hex within two — you pick the direction *and* the distance, not a fixed hop down one of six axes. Thrusters carry you straight through bodies and threatened ground, but never through bulkheads and never around a corner: the lane has to be a straight shot. Armed targets ring in cyan, brightest at full range.
- **Deflect** as an adjacent machine swings: the hit is negated and the attacker overloads for a cycle — then counterstrike for double. A deflect into empty air wastes the turn.
- **Exposed cores**: machines have facing; strike from the rear arc for critical damage.
- **Repair cells** restore integrity but injecting one costs the turn — patching yourself inside a telegraph gets you exactly what you deserve.
- **Repair bays** heal, refill and fabricate upgrades for cores — but docking reinitializes the sector, and everything you killed comes back. Only the five-sector prologue has one. Foundry maps carry no bay: whatever you drop in with is what you clear the sector with.
- **Fall**, and the cores you carried scatter where you died — persisted across runs. Your next frame can reclaim them from the wreck. Fall again first, and they're gone.

## The Arsenal

Loot works like a classic ARPG (Path of Exile style). Five equipment slots — **Weapon**, **Plating**, **Sensor**, **Drive**, **Utility** — and every item is a **base type** (which fixes its slot and implicit modifier) at a **rarity**:

- **Normal** (white): just the base and its implicit. Raw material.
- **Magic** (blue): up to 1 prefix + 1 suffix.
- **Rare** (yellow): up to 2 prefixes + 2 suffixes, with a generated name.
- **Unique** (orange): hand-authored items with fixed, oversized effects — and lore.

Every base type's **implicit** is always present, at any rarity, and rolls within a range rather than a fixed number — PoE2-style, not PoE1: two plain Bulkhead Segments won't carry identical integrity bonuses, and the range itself widens the deeper the sector, so a T15 drop's implicit alone can beat a T1 drop's. It never eats into the prefix/suffix budget above.

Prefixes carry raw power (damage, integrity, rear-strike, repair output, core yield); suffixes carry utility (power reserve, dash and deflect costs, sensor range, heal-on-kill). Modifier values roll in five **tiers**, and deeper sectors roll higher tiers. One modifier per stat per item.

**Currency orbs** are the crafting system, straight from the PoE playbook: **Transmutation** (Normal → Magic), **Augmentation** (add a mod to Magic), **Alchemy** (Normal → Rare), **Regal** (Magic → Rare, keeping its mods), **Exalted** (add a mod to Rare), **Chaos** (swap one mod on a Rare for a new one), **Blessed** (reroll an item's implicit, at your current depth — so early gear catches up). Blessed is the odd one out: it only touches the implicit, never affixes, so unlike the other five it works on any rarity, Uniques included — the affix-crafting restrictions on Uniques don't apply to it. Orbs come from caches and elite kills. Swapping gear or crafting is free in peace — under hostile eyes it costs your turn.

Corrupted terminals fabricate **corrupted rares**: strong rolls with a downside modifier baked in, and corruption seals the item against every orb. No take-backs.

The gear panel (`B`) is tabbed — **Equipped | Backpack | Keys | Supplies**, with live counts on each tab and number keys `1`–`4` to switch. The backpack sorts by slot (with section headers) and best rarity first; keys sort deepest-tier first, crafted ahead of plain. The Keys tab only appears once the Foundry is unlocked. Every backpack card carries a **Compare** button that diffs its derived stats against whatever's currently equipped in that slot — dmg, costs, integrity, power, and every other gear-affected number, colored green for an upgrade and red for a downgrade, with anything unchanged left out — so you can tell whether a pickup is worth equipping before committing the (free) gear-change turn.

Nothing in the backpack is dead weight: every unequipped item can be **salvaged for cores** on the spot. Value follows the same axes the loot chase does — a rarity floor (Normal 5, Magic 15, Rare 40, Unique 120) plus a bonus per affix tier, so a deep-tier rare is worth carrying home; corrupted parts fetch half. Rare and Unique salvage arms on the first tap and sells on the second, so a fat-fingered tap can't vaporize the chase item. Like all gear-fiddling, salvaging is free in peace and costs the turn under hostile eyes.

Sector Keys salvage too, from the Keys tab. Value is priced off what fabricating an equivalent key costs — half the tier's fab cost as a floor, plus a share per mod carried — so it never quite pays to fabricate a key and immediately break it back down, while a heavily-crafted rare key (multiple orbs sunk into it) pays out well. Rare keys arm the same way rare items do.

## The Foundry (endgame)

Kill the OVERSEER once and the game changes shape: the five-sector descent becomes a one-time prologue, and the **Foundry** opens — an endless overworld hex map of sealed sector nodes spreading outward from your home dock, **the Bay**. Your character is now persistent: frame, gear, currency, and the map itself survive death and browser restarts (saved in `localStorage`).

**Nothing is lost to a refresh**: the full game state — prologue floor or keyed sector, mid-fight — checkpoints after every turn, and the menu offers **Resume run** when you come back. Death and extraction retire the checkpoint; refreshing mid-sector no longer costs the key.

- **The overworld is a landscape, not a node grid.** Terrain is derived deterministically from the atlas seed, so the world is infinite and saves stay tiny: impassable **slag ridges** and glowing **coolant channels** carve the map into valleys and chokepoints, **biomes come in contiguous belts** you can see and traverse, and passable ground is either a sealed sector **site** or open **field**. Purging a sector flood-reveals the whole pocket of landscape it connects to — fields cascade, sites on the pocket's edge become your new frontier, and terrain decides the shape of it. The map view shows the landscape continuing two hexes past everything you know, so you can plan routes around a ridge line before you get there.
- **Nodes have no tier — the key is the tier.** Overworld nodes carry only a biome; socket a **Sector Key** into any frontier node and the sector generates from that node's seed at the *key's* tier, exactly like a PoE2 waystone sets the map level. Cleared nodes remember the tier they were purged at. Keys drop from cleared sectors (always at least one, at tier or tier+1, occasionally pre-modified), and the Bay fabricates plain ones for cores.
- **Keys are craftable items.** The same currency orbs that craft gear craft keys: Transmutation/Alchemy on a plain key, Augmentation/Regal/Exalted/Chaos up the chain — Magic keys hold 2 modifiers, Rares hold 4 (and get generated names). Every key mod trades danger for **bonus loot quantity**: *Swarming* (+50% packs), *Overcharged* (+1 damage), *Armored* (+30% integrity), *Primed* (+1 Prime), *Darkened* (−2 sensor range), *Volatile* (machines detonate on death — 1 damage adjacent, fully deterministic), *Rusted* (repair cells heal −3). Quantity adds caches, orbs from Primes, and extra key-drop chance.
- Every keyed sector has a **purge objective**: kill all its Prime units. Purging a node marks it cleared and reveals its neighbors — the map literally grows as you go.
- **Biomes look and fight differently.** Each biome has its own palette and its own *shape* of space, generated deterministically per sector: **Scrapyards** are rust-toned debris fields — scattered clumps, chaotic sightlines, brawler territory; **Rail Depots** are ochre lane systems — long corridors with punched doorways that make railers and mortars scarier because of the map; **Bastion Lines** are blue-steel chamber complexes — rooms joined by chokepoint doors where a bulwark holding a doorway is a real problem; **Archive Vaults** are violet storage halls — ordered aisles with the loot deep in the shelving. A **tier tint** shifts every palette darker and hotter as keys get deeper, so a T12 sector reads dangerous before you check the HUD — and floors stay dark on every palette so the red/amber telegraphs never lose legibility. The prologue inherits the themes floor by floor. Layouts are always pruned to one connected region, and a generator that walls off too much falls back to open cavern noise, so sectors never generate cramped.
- **Death costs the key, not the character.** Your frame is rebuilt at the Bay, but the cores you carried stay in the node as a wreck — socket another key into that node to reclaim them. Die after the sector's already flipped `cleared` (last Prime down, still walking to Extract) and there's no node left to hold a wreck — the cores just ride back with you instead of being destroyed. Extraction from a purged sector is free and repairs you fully; attrition lives inside sectors, not between them.
- **No bay inside a map.** The mid-sector repair bay that heals, refills and re-shops in the prologue doesn't exist in keyed sectors — no free heal, no docking to reinitialize the fight and try it fresh. Whatever integrity, cells and power you carry into a map is what you clear it with; prep at the Bay (or the Fabricate uplink) before you go in, not partway through.
- **Keyed sectors have no drop shaft — Extract is the only way out.** A green **"SECTOR PURGED — hit Extract"** banner stays on screen the whole time a sector is cleared but you haven't left, so it's never easy to miss the button and wander a finished sector looking for stairs that were never there.
- **Gates band the climb to T15 — and every band has its own guardian.** Purge a sector at your current cap (T4 to start) and a red **gate node** surfaces past the frontier. Arm it with a full band-tier key to enter an open boss arena — room to roam, a few pillars, one guardian. Kill it and the cap jumps four tiers (T4 → T8 → T12 → T15), with band keys and orbs as spoils; key mods apply to the gate too, juicing the guardian and the reward. The ladder teaches a new verb per band: the **SENTINEL** (T4) with its coolant-gap slam and alternating sweep; the **WARDEN** (T8), siege artillery whose barrage tracks where you *stood*, chained crossfire axes, and a frontal shield that makes flanking mandatory; the **CRUCIBLE** (T12), whose forge-wave rolls outward ring by ring — the cold spot is *inside* it, against the hull — venting six-spoke bursts up close and fabricating rippers mid-fight, capped at 4 concurrently alive so a fight that runs long doesn't snowball into an unmanageable swarm. Which guardian wakes at which band is one config table (`levelGen.gateBossByBand`).
- **Past the ladder, tiers never end.** Once the CRUCIBLE falls and the cap reaches T15, the drop clamp opens: a tier-n sector can always yield a key up to **tier n+1** (`levelGen.sector.keyDropAheadPostLadder`) — map 16 hides map 17, map 40 hides map 41, forever. The Bay still only fabricates up to T15, so the deep climb is *sustained by drops*: keep hitting the ~1-in-3 tier-up roll (or bank spare deep keys) to push, fall back to fabricated T15s to rebuild if the well runs dry. Enemy integrity, damage, pack density and Prime counts all run on open formulas with no ceiling — every build has a tier that finally stops it, and that tier is the score.
- **The Foundry Heart is the summit.** When the last gate falls and the cap tops out, a violet **apex node** surfaces: the **FORGE-PRIME**, behind a T15 key, in a wider arena. It runs the whole ladder's curriculum in phases — slam and charge, then shielded crossfire, then rolling waves with a vent window one beat stingier. Every kill pays lattice points and a **guaranteed Unique**, and the Heart always grows back somewhere on the frontier — the one fight that's always worth the key it costs, and the endgame's repeatable pinnacle.
- **Boss patterns you dodge by stepping IN.** The SENTINEL's long windups have safe spots *inside* the marked pattern: its two-turn **field slam** covers everything within three hexes *except* three coolant-gap pockets — the play is to stand in a hole surrounded by red. Its **alternating sweep** fires the red lanes first and the amber lanes one turn later, so the dodge is into amber, then back into the lane that just fired. It line-charges to reposition and overheats after every third attack, same rhythm language as the OVERSEER. All of it fully deterministic and telegraphed.
- **Foundry Anomalies** are node events, rolled once and permanently the moment a frontier node is revealed (~28% of nodes carry one) — the key you socket only scales how hard and how rich it runs, never which event it is. Each tests a different verb and pays a different currency:
  - **Fabricator Surge** (⚒) — a dormant machine you interact with to wake. It runs a scripted 4-wave production line over ~12 cycles; every arrival telegraphs amber one full cycle before it spawns, so nothing is a jumpscare. Hold your ground within 3 hexes when the run ends and it vends a Surge Cache — a bonus item roll plus orbs. Wander off and the cache just seals.
  - **Timed Vault** (◫) — 1–3 upgraded-quality chests behind a countdown: the moment any of them enters your sensor range, `VAULT LOCKDOWN` arms and the door welds shut in 9 cycles. Reach it in time or the unopened chests are gone for good — pure route-optimization under pressure.
  - **Salvage Convoy** (▸) — unarmed hauler drones cross the sector on a fixed, precomputed route with armed escorts riding along; haulers never fight back, so the puzzle is pure hex-tactics — cut the path before they clear the far side. Kill one for cargo currency and a real shot at a Sector Key; let it through and its loot is gone.
  - **Corrupted Zone** (☣) — a static risk pocket, no new machinery: anything born inside hits harder and detonates on death, and anything looted inside rolls from deeper in the tier bands. Where you choose to fight becomes the loot decision.


## The power curve

Power is meant to live in gear — and, past the prologue, in the **frame lattice**: a permanent upgrade tree that replaced the shop's old flat frame upgrades (which capped out after the prologue and turned into a wall of MAX buttons). The lattice can't be bought with cores at all. **Points come from Foundry milestones** — 1 per first-time sector purge, 2 per fallen SENTINEL gate — so it keeps paying out exactly as long as you keep climbing, the way PoE2's atlas passives do. Old saves are migrated whole: every core spent on the retired upgrades is refunded, the stats those ranks granted are stripped back out, and every sector already purged grants its point retroactively.

Three branches — about twenty nodes each, 61 in all — grow from the frame: **Chassis** (integrity, repair cells, the deflector), **Servos** (damage, rear-strikes, dash and power), **Systems** (core yield, sensors, loot). Each runs a spine of small stat nodes punctuated by three **notables**, hangs **side twigs** off the spine, and is capped by a tradeoff **keystone** — past which a **prism socket** fans into a tip cluster whose ring closes on a cluster notable (reachable through either facet). The smalls speak the same stat vocabulary as gear; the notables gate real combat-code branches you can't get anywhere else: *Reactive Plating* (a successful deflect refunds its 2 power), *Aegis Long-Field* (deflect catches strikes at **any** range, not just adjacent), *Momentum Reclaimer* (a rear-strike kill vents 2 power back), *Executioner Logic* (+1 damage vs overloaded machines, folded in before the riposte double), *Salvage Rites* (Primes drop an extra orb), *Deep-Cycle Scanners* (loot rolls its affixes 2 tiers deeper than the sector it fell in). The keystones commit a build: *Monolith Chassis* (+6 integrity, dash costs +1), *Glass Core* (+1 dmg/+1 rear-strike, −6 integrity), *Greed Circuit* (+40% core yield/+1 sensor, deflect costs +1). **Only one keystone can be active at a time** — they're a real tradeoff, not a checklist, so taking a different one means removing the current one (and its whole tip cluster, tip-inward) first; the other two branches' clusters simply stay locked behind a keystone you'll never install alongside your own. Removal is otherwise free but only from the tip of a branch inward, so respeccing to chase a different notable is always a couple of taps, never a punishment. The lattice belongs to the endgame frame — it never applies to a prologue run.

A fourth, tiny **root cluster** sits at the hub itself, independent of all three branches: three mutually-exclusive **special attacks**, PoE2-ascendancy-style — no prerequisite, so any one of them is a legal first point spent, regardless of which branch you go on to specialize in. *Overload Slam* hits and staggers every adjacent machine at once. *Rail Charge* punches a straight lane, striking everything on the path before landing on the far side — a moving cleave. *Barrage Volley* fires the same lane damage at range without moving, the one way a melee-only loadout gets reach. All three share one power cost and damage multiplier (`combat.special` in config.js) so tuning the whole verb is a single-number edit. Whichever one is installed shows up as a sixth action-bar button (`[S]`) — Slam resolves on the tap, Charge and Barrage drop into the same click-a-highlighted-hex targeting mode Dash already uses. Swapping to a different special is always free, since nothing ever hangs a child node off a leaf with no branch beneath it. Each attack gets its own signature motion in the special-attack teal, distinct from a plain hit's flash-and-number: Slam is a ring expanding outward from the frame, Charge is a streak that traces the lane as you cross it, Barrage is a beam that lights the full lane at once and fades — three different shapes for three different verbs, not the same effect scaled up. The base **Thruster dash** everyone has gets the same streak treatment in cyan instead of teal, so it now reads as its own motion rather than an ordinary walk covering more ground.

It looks the part, too: a pannable, pinch-zoomable radial constellation with the frame's hub emblem at the center and the three limbs winding outward 120° apart — hex nodes sized by kind, ringed notables in amber, keystones in rose, prism sockets in violet showing their empty seat until a gem is set, specials in teal, the allocated path glowing along its curved edges over star-dusted dark. Each limb has its own silhouette on purpose: servos runs long and blade-narrow, chassis sits stocky and wide with plating hung off both sides, systems sprawls in a meandering scanner-web, and the root cluster is just three short leaves fanned right at the hub. Names stay off the tree; tapping a node brings its name, effect, and the install/remove action up into the detail bar under the header.

The bay is still a real vendor, in four sections:

- **Frame lattice** — the tree above, opened from the shop (points shown on the row); the prologue shows it dormant until the OVERSEER falls.
- **Restock** — Shock Darts (40 cores) and Power Cells (60), repeatable forever and otherwise unobtainable outside chest luck.
- **Currency orbs** — every orb is purchasable at a premium over drop rates: Transmutation 30, Augmentation 50, Alchemy 90, Regal 140, Blessed 120, Chaos 250, Exalted 400. Cores flow straight into the crafting chase.
- **Prototype fabrication** — the gamble vendor: 350 cores buys a blind-rolled **Rare** in a slot of your choice, with modifiers rolled one past your atlas tier cap — the same affix bands the deepest sector you can key would drop. The endgame core sink.

And in the Foundry, the shop is no longer locked behind docking: a **Fabricate** button sits next to Gear on the overworld — a remote uplink to the Bay fabricator from anywhere on the map. Together with salvage this closes the loop: strip unwanted loot for cores, spend cores on orbs, prototypes, keys and tools.

**Enemies scale to match a 15-tier climb**: integrity grows ~6.9× from T1 to T15 (up from ~4.5×, then ~5.9×), damage now adds +1 every 2 tiers starting from T1 itself instead of waiting until T3 (closing a gap where the first two tiers got zero damage scaling at all — every tier is +1 harder than before, top to bottom), an extra Prime unit joins at T1/T4/T7/T10/T13 (spread evenly across the whole climb instead of clustering late) — since the purge objective only requires killing Primes, more of them at once is what actually forces multiple simultaneous attackers on a player who's otherwise routing around trash packs one at a time — and pack density grows with tier (doubled from the original coefficient). **Gear has a reason to keep chasing past T4**: prefix and suffix modifiers now roll five tiers instead of three — tier 4 only starts appearing at T8+, tier 5 at T12+ — so there are affix rolls that flatly cannot exist on early gear. **And past T15 gear never stops growing**: a rolled affix's magnitude scales linearly with depth beyond the ladder (`items.affixDeepScaling`, the same shape implicits already scale by) — the tier-5 tables are exact through T15, then a T35 drop rolls roughly double the numbers a T15 drop can. Deep loot only falls in deep maps, so the item chase and the tier climb pull each other forever.

A dedicated harness (`tests/balance-smoke.js`) measures this instead of eyeballing it: it drops reference characters into real keyed sectors at T1/T4/T8/T12/T15 and reads the actual scaled combat stats the game computes, then asserts a bare frame falls behind the curve while a tier-appropriately-geared one stays in a playable time-to-kill / hits-to-die band at every tier tested — and that even the real achievable lattice ceiling (every node one keystone's build can reach — the other two branches' clusters are locked out, since keystones are exclusive) on top of tier gear stays inside a sane band, with the tree's total flat-stat budget bounded so milestones supplement the gear chase rather than replace it. A real T9 export surfaced the problem this table now guards against: a fully-invested character (all three keystones stacked, pre-exclusivity) was taking zero damage across an entire sector — the lattice's flat power had grown to dwarf gear's own contribution, and nothing about the curve clawed it back. Run this suite any time a balance change lands.

## Balance config

Everything on this page — enemy stats and their tier-scaling formula, prologue floor tables, Foundry sector-generation coefficients (pack density, elite bump tiers, chest/terminal odds, key-drop odds, gate jump size, every Sector Key modifier), the whole economy (restocks, orb prices, the prototype gamble, the key-fabrication curve, item and key salvage values, plus the retired shop upgrades kept purely as migration-refund data), the entire frame lattice (points per purge and per gate, and every node — its branch, edges, stat magnitudes, and each notable's or special's mechanic key and power), the root special attacks' shared power cost/range/damage multiplier, item/affix/rarity tables (base type damage/costs, every implicit's roll range and the coefficient that scales it with depth, every prefix and suffix pool with its five tier magnitudes and — currently unused — the slot restrictions each could carry, corrupted-downside mods, the depth bands that gate tier 4/5 affixes, uniques, crafting caps), small combat constants (dash range, sensor range and its floor, dash/deflect cost bounds, repair-cell heal, the detonation damage shared by the Volatile key mod and a Corrupted Zone kill), and node-event tuning (Anomaly density and the odds of each kind, Fabricator Surge wave count/interval/soul bonus, Timed Vault lockdown cycles, Salvage Convoy hauler count/entry delay, Corrupted Zone radius/damage) — lives as plain data in **`config.js`**, loaded before the game itself. Retuning any of it is a numbers edit, not a code edit.

It's a `<script>`-assigned object rather than a real `.json` file on purpose: this game runs by opening `index.html` directly, no server, and a browser can't `fetch()` a local file over `file://` — which is also how the whole test suite runs. `config.js` gets the same treatment either way: `rl.js` validates its shape at boot and fails loudly, with a console error for every missing field and a red banner across the page, if something's wrong — there's no silent fallback to a hardcoded default, so the file is genuinely the one place these numbers live. `tests/config-smoke.js` proves the wiring: that the numbers rl.js uses actually come from `config.js` rather than coincidentally matching a copy still baked into the formulas, and that the validator catches a broken config with a real, specific error.

What's *not* in there: item/affix *identity* — slot assignment, names, flavor text, and the cleave/reach flags that gate real attack-code branches (a three-hex cleave arc, a two-hex reach line) rather than tune a magnitude. Those stay in `rl.js` as the "what this thing IS" half of the split, alongside terrain generators, boss attack patterns, and AI, which are behavior rather than balance dials and always will be.

Every affix in `config.js` also carries a `slots` field, currently `null` on every entry — meaning any base type can roll any prefix or suffix, which is the real behavior today. The read path already respects it (`rollAffix` filters the pool by the item's slot whenever `slots` isn't null), so gating an affix to specific slots later is a config edit, not a code change.

## Deploy badge

A small, non-interactive footer note — `deployed <date/time>` — sits at the bottom of the intro/menu page, below the debug export/import links. It lives inside `#menu` in the DOM, so it hides itself the instant that overlay does — no separate show/hide logic, just ordinary page flow. There's no build step or CI on this static site to bake in a real deploy timestamp, so `DEPLOY_TIME` in `rl.js` (right next to `GAME_VERSION`) is stamped by hand on every push to `main`. Since GitHub Pages serves this repo directly with no build stage, the last commit to `main` *is* the last deployment, so the two are always the same moment — bump it as the last edit before every commit that ships.

## Debug export

Every screen has a way to export the exact game state as JSON, for bug reports: the 🐞 icon in the topbar during play, **Export debug state** on the main menu and the death screen. It bundles campaign meta (deaths, wins, best floor), the full Foundry profile (character, atlas, keys) if unlocked, and a fresh mid-run checkpoint if one applies — everything needed to reproduce what you were seeing. **Import debug state** on the main menu loads one of these files back in (overwriting local storage) and reloads the page, so a state exported from your browser reproduces exactly in mine.

The export also carries `lastOutcome`: a full account of the last level you died or won, captured the instant it happened — prologue floor or Foundry sector, gate, or the apex; tier, biome/boss, the enemy that landed the killing blow (or `null` on a win), turn and kill counts, cores held, hp/max hp, the key's rarity and mods if one was involved, and your equipped loadout at the time. It's not a full multi-run history — only the single most recent death or win, overwritten by whichever happens next — but it's not just the final moment either: `actions` is the step-by-step log of that entire attempt, in order — every move, dash, wait, deflect (attempt and hit-or-miss), repair, attack and the hits/kills it landed, hit taken, item picked up, and floor descended, each stamped with the turn it happened on. It resets fresh each time you jack into a sector (or start the prologue over), so it's scoped to the one attempt that ended in this outcome, and it isn't cut short until you enter a *new* sector — die right after purging one and both the win and the death that followed are in the same account. Paired with the rest of the export, that's enough to reconstruct *exactly* what happened on a specific run, not just where you ended up. No separate export needed: it rides in the same file, top-level as `lastOutcome` and duplicated inside `campaignMeta.lastOutcome` (its source of truth), so one export always has both the current state and how you got there.

## The descent

Five sectors of procedural machine caverns under fog of war. **Scrappers** run a broken loop and swing. **Rail Drones** rake an entire lane (their slugs don't check for friendlies) and must recharge between shots. **Bulwarks** absorb every frontal hit with a shield emitter — flank them in the beat after they swing, or deflect to overload the field. **Mortars** arc charges *over walls*: a seven-hex blast, two cycles out — cover is no cover, keep moving. **Crushers** telegraph a two-cycle shockwave ring and lock up afterward; **Rippers** cover two hexes a turn and hit hard. Sectors 2–4 hide a glowing Prime unit and a **corrupted terminal** offering corrupted rare gear — guaranteed power with a downside baked in. In Sector 5, **the OVERSEER**: cleaves, line charges, a fabricator slam below half integrity — and after every third attack it has to vent heat. Learn the cycle.

**Hover (or long-press) any machine** to scan its integrity, damage, current state, and how to beat it.

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| Tap / click a hex | move, or strike an adjacent machine |
| Tap your own hex / `Space` | vent heat (+2 power) |
| Dash button / `R`, then a highlighted hex | thruster dash |
| Deflect `F` · Repair `H` · Gear `B` | you know what these do |
| Tap far ground | cautious auto-move (stops when contacts appear) |
| Drag / pinch | pan / zoom |

## Verified by scripted playtests

Determinism makes the design testable, and it is — in headless Chromium:

- A combat-and-encounter suite covers the core rules: telegraphs hit exactly the marked hexes and nothing else, dashes clear them, deflects overload, counterstrikes double, rear hits crit, repair cells get punished mid-telegraph, crushers are punishable after slamming, bulwark shields block frontal hits until flanked or deflected, mortar blasts are outrunnable, wrecks persist and are reclaimable, drop shafts are reachable across seeds, and the OVERSEER telegraphs and overheats on cycle.
- A checkpoint suite (`tests/checkpoint-smoke.js`) reloads the page mid-run and proves nothing is lost: the menu offers Resume, a resumed prologue matches turn/floor/seed/hp exactly and stays playable, a resumed keyed sector keeps its node and enemies without double-spending the key, death and extraction both clear the checkpoint.
- A debug-export suite (`tests/debug-export-smoke.js`) proves the bundle a player would send us actually reproduces: it captures the right shape and campaign meta at the prologue, a null run checkpoint while just browsing the overworld, a fresh sector checkpoint the moment one applies, and — the real test — importing that JSON into a brand-new page restores the exact turn, seed, hp and sector, with malformed or foreign JSON rejected cleanly.
